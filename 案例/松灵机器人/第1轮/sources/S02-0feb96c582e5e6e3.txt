## FAQ

What is PiPER and what can it be used for?

PiPER is a lightweight 6-DoF robotic arm for robotics research, education, physical AI development, and light manipulation & automation applications. With the right end-effector, PiPER can support grasping, assembly, object sorting, and robot data collection.

What are PiPER’s key specifications?

The standard PiPER provides a 1.5kg payload, a 626mm working reach, ±0.1mm repeatability, and a 4.2kg arm body.
Does PiPER support Python, ROS, and robotic arm simulation?

Yes. PiPER supports Python SDKs, ROS 1, ROS 2, and URDF models for Gazebo and Isaac Sim, ideal for robotic arm development, algorithm validation, and sim-to-real research.

How to use PiPER robotic arm?

PiPER supports drag teaching, offline trajectory recording and replay, PC-based control, and programmed robotic arm motion.

Can I Run VLA or World Models on PiPER?
Yes. PiPER can be integrated with VLA models such as π0/π0.5, OpenVLA, SmolVLA, NVIDIA Isaac GR00T, and Gemini Robotics to execute AI-generated actions on a real robotic arm. It can also work with world-model platforms such as NVIDIA Cosmos and Google Genie for simulation, prediction, data generation, and policy evaluation.AI agents such as Jev and GPT 6 can also serve as the decision-making layer, sending tasks and action commands to PiPER.
Integration is supported through PiPER’s Python and ROS interfaces, with model adaptation for camera inputs, action mapping, calibration, end-effectors, and robot control.
Can PiPER be integrated with other robotics platforms?

Yes. PiPER can be integrated with mobile robot bases, quadruped robots, and other platforms to build complete systems including mobile manipulation.

What must be completed before using PiPER?
Before operating PiPER, securely mount the arm, verify the power and CAN connections, calibrate the joint zero positions, and set the J6 zero point after installing an end-effector. Users should also confirm that the workspace is clear and complete a safety assessment. PiPER does not include automatic obstacle avoidance or sensing.
